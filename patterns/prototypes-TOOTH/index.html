Frequently Asked Questions

  • Home

  • FAQ
  • Contact Us

Is a written consent required from the teachers?

No, we are not collecting any personal information from teachers who participates in the study.

Is written consent required from the students?

No, students are not directly participating in the study. There are no surveys for the students. The study is about training the teachers.

Are teachers and students given kits?

Yes. Participating teachers and their students are given an oral-hygiene kits. Teachers are also given educational booklets, and teaching materials.

How are participants paid?

Compensation is provided through a ClinCard (reloadable gift card).

Total compensation?

Up to $300 for completing all training sessions and follow-up surveys

Is there compensation for my time?

Day 1 1-hour training and surveys $100
Day 7 Surveys (online) $25
3 Months Follow-up seminars via zoom (30 minutes) and surveys (online) $25
6 Months Follow-up seminars via zoom (30 minutes) and surveys (online) $25
12 Months Follow-up seminars via zoom (30 minutes) and surveys (online) $100
Bonus* *$25 additional compensation for completing all surveys $25
Total Honorarium $300

TOOTH Project

Teaching Oral Health to Educators in Elementary Schools in Texas

  • Home
  • Our Team
  • Training
  • FAQ
  • Contact Us

The TOOTH Project provides training to teachers that will improve their students’ oral health.

This study is important because:

  • Extremely high level of dental diseases in Texas
  • Almost 50% of kindergarten children has cavities
  • 1 in 6 children have untreated tooth decay

Students might visit a dentist only once a year or over a year, which is why elementary school teachers are important as teachers work with students on a regular basis, and educating students at an early age helps them establish good healthy behaviors that will last a lifetime.

In our survery, we are evaluating:

  • Teachers’ current knowledge of oral health
  • Teachers' opinions of the training
  • Teachers' opinions of the students’ curriculum

Dental disease is caused by dental plaque (gum disease and cavities) and a sugary diet (cavities). The impact of oral diseases in children can result in pain and suffering, missing school, parents missing work, malnutrition, and aggressive behavior.

We can prevent oral diseases by:

  • Proper oral hygiene
  • Controlling what you eat (sugary products)
  • Fluoride (toothpaste and dentist)
