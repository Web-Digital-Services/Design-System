About Us

Our Center brings together a dedicated core team, an engaged advisory board, and accomplished faculty who share a commitment to advancing infectious disease research. From clinical trials to community-based studies, we foster collaboration across disciplines to generate innovative insights and practical solutions that address the health needs of South Texas and inform care worldwide.

Who We Are

The South Texas region faces a disproportionate burden of chronic infectious diseases—including HIV, tuberculosis, endemic fungal infections, and Long COVID—that impact both urban and rural communities. These challenges are intensified by coexisting conditions such as diabetes, cardiovascular disease, cancer, and substance use disorders.

Our Center brings together UT Health San Antonio’s world-class researchers and community partners to develop innovative strategies for prevention, diagnosis, and treatment. With expertise in epidemiology, diagnostics, clinical research, and implementation science, our faculty are national leaders in infectious disease research, including groundbreaking COVID-19 and HIV studies.

By partnering with the communities most affected, we aim to transform care locally in South Texas while advancing solutions with national and global impact.

Research Focus

Our research focuses on chronic infectious diseases that disproportionately impact South Texas, including HIV, tuberculosis, fungal infections, and Long COVID. We design and conduct clinical trials, vaccine studies, behavioral interventions, and community-based research to improve prevention, diagnosis, and treatment. By addressing these challenges in the context of coexisting health conditions, we aim to deliver solutions that improve lives locally and globally.

Disease areas being studied

  • Long COVID
  • Tuberculosis
  • HIV
  • Endemic Fungal Infections

Our expertise is not limited to the above conditions, so please reach out if you have additional areas of interest."

Recent Discoveries

Our Center is at the forefront of advancing care for chronic infectious diseases. From leading international COVID-19 therapeutic trials that informed Long COVID treatments, to groundbreaking work in HIV and vaccine studies, our team has contributed to discoveries with both local and global impact. We are also developing innovative diagnostics, testing new prevention strategies, and partnering with South Texas communities to ensure research directly improves lives.

A Double-blind, Randomized Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Bezisterim (NE3107) in Adults With Long COVID

Principal Investigator: Thomas Patterson, MD
Conditions: Long COVID-19

Purpose

Long COVID is a condition where debilitating symptoms can persist for months after a COVID-19 infection. This study aims to evaluate the effects of NE3107 on several neurological symptoms reported in people with Long COVID including difficulty concentrating or remembering things ("brain fog") and fatigue. Researchers will compare NE3107 to a placebo (a look-alike substance that contains no drug) to see if NE3107 works to treat neurocognitive and fatigue symptoms of long COVID.

Eligibility Criteria

Inclusion Criteria:

  1. 18 years to 64 years of age
  2. diagnosed with Long COVID with symptoms of fatigue and brain fog for at least 3 months
  3. agree to use birth control measures
  4. provide voluntary consent
  5. willing to allow blood collection
  6. pass all screening tests and procedures

Exclusion Criteria:

  1. has received a COVID-19 vaccination within 30 days
  2. previous admission to the intensive care unit for COVID-19
  3. medical history of major mental or physical illness prior to COVID-19 infection

About Participation

Q: What is clinical research?
A: Clinical research is the process of studying health and disease in people to find new ways to prevent, diagnose, and treat medical conditions. Research studies help us learn what works best and ensure new treatments are safe and effective.

Q: Why should I consider participating in a research study?
A: By joining a study, you contribute to discoveries that can improve health for your community and beyond. Many participants also gain access to new treatments, closer health monitoring, and the chance to work with a dedicated research team.

Q: Do I need to have a specific medical condition to join a study?
A: Not always. Some studies are for people with specific conditions, while others need healthy volunteers. Each study has its own eligibility criteria, which we’ll review with you.

Q: What types of research does your Center conduct?
A: We focus on chronic infectious diseases that affect South Texas, including HIV, tuberculosis, fungal infections, and Long COVID. Our studies include clinical trials of new treatments, vaccine research, behavioral interventions, and community-based health studies.

Safety & Confidentiality

Q: How is my safety protected during a study?
A: Your safety is our top priority. All studies are reviewed and approved by ethics committees, and you’ll be closely monitored by trained medical staff throughout the process.

Q: Who oversees the ethical conduct of your research?
A: Every study is reviewed by an Institutional Review Board (IRB), which ensures research is conducted ethically and safely.

Q: Will my personal information remain confidential?
A: Yes. We follow strict federal and institutional privacy rules to protect your information. Only approved members of the research team will have access to your study data.

Logistics

Q: How do I find out which studies are currently enrolling?
A: Visit our Research Studies page or contact our team directly. We’ll be happy to share information about ongoing studies and check if you’re eligible.

Q: What happens during a typical research visit?
A: It depends on the study, but visits may include a health screening, answering questionnaires, lab tests, or receiving a treatment. We’ll explain everything in advance and answer all your questions.

Q: Will I be compensated for my time or travel?
A: Many of our studies offer compensation for your time, travel, or other expenses. Details vary by study and will be provided during the consent process.

Q: Do I have to come to your Center, or can some studies be done at home?
A: Some studies require in-person visits, while others may include phone calls, online surveys, or home-based participation. We’ll let you know what options are available.

Rights & Commitment

Q: Can I stop participating in a study once I start?
A: Yes. Participation is completely voluntary, and you can withdraw at any time without penalty or loss of medical care.

Q: What are my rights as a research participant?
A: You have the right to be fully informed, to ask questions, to withdraw at any time, and to have your privacy protected. Your rights will be explained in detail before you agree to join any study.

Q: What should I do if I have questions or concerns during a study?
A: You can reach out to the research team at any time. We will provide you with direct contact information before your study begins.

Community Engagement

Q: How does your Center work with South Texas communities?
A: We partner closely with local organizations, clinics, and community leaders to ensure our research addresses real health needs and respects community values.

Q: How does participating in research help improve health in my community?
A: Every study helps us better understand infectious diseases and develop new solutions. This knowledge directly benefits South Texas and contributes to national and global health.

Q: Can community organizations partner with your Center?
A: Yes! We welcome partnerships with schools, clinics, nonprofits, and other organizations that share our mission. Contact us to explore opportunities.

Other Practical Questions

Q: Do you provide services in Spanish or other languages?
A: Yes. Many of our staff are bilingual, and interpreter services are available to ensure you can participate comfortably in your preferred language.

Q: Where are you located, and is parking available?
A: We are located at [insert building/UT Health San Antonio address]. Visitor parking is available nearby, and we will provide details when you schedule your first visit.

Q: Who can I contact if I’m interested in learning more?
A: You can complete the form on the Contact Us page. Our research team will be happy to answer your questions!

Join Our Core Faculty

We invite faculty who are passionate about advancing research on chronic infectious diseases to become part of our Core Faculty. By joining, you’ll gain access to dedicated resources, collaborative opportunities, and support to help your research thrive—while contributing to a Center that is making a real impact in South Texas and beyond.

  • Benefits for Core Faculty

    • Formal recognition as part of a collaborative, mission-driven Center.
    • Priority access to Center research infrastructure, including study coordination and regulatory support. Opportunities to lead or participate in multi-disciplinary research projects.
    • Eligibility for pilot funding, seed grants, or other internal support opportunities (if available).
    • Enhanced visibility through Center communications, events, and website profiles.
    • Networking and collaboration with community partners, advisory board members, and other faculty.
    • Professional development support, including guidance on grants, publications, and presentations.

  • Expectations of Core Faculty

    • Actively contribute to the Center’s research mission through participation in studies, mentoring, or collaborative projects.
    • Attend Center meetings, seminars, or events when possible. Share research updates and publications to highlight the Center’s impact.
    • Serve as mentors or collaborators for junior faculty, trainees, or community partners when appropriate.
    • Represent the Center in a positive and professional manner across institutional and community settings.

  • Services Offered by the Center

    Our team is here to help faculty succeed in their research endeavors. Services include:

    • Clinical Trial Support – From design to implementation, we can run your clinical trial with experienced staff, facilities, and regulatory guidance.
    • Abstract & Manuscript Support – Assistance with writing, editing, and submission to conferences or journals.
    • Grant Development Assistance – Guidance on proposal preparation, budgets, and submission processes.
    • Community Engagement – Connections with South Texas communities to strengthen recruitment and impact.
    • Training & Mentorship – Support for faculty, staff, and trainees in clinical and translational research methods.
    • Collaborative Opportunities – Even if you’re not a Core Faculty member, we welcome partnerships on projects that align with our mission.

Choose Us for Your Next Clinical Trial or Research Study

  • Experienced Team – Core Faculty and research staff with national and international trial leadership experience. Regulatory & Operational Expertise – Full support with study design, regulatory submissions, IRB coordination, and site management.
  • Access to Diverse Populations – Reach South Texas communities, including underserved and high-burden populations, for rapid recruitment and meaningful outcomes.
  • Comprehensive Trial Capabilities – Inpatient and outpatient trials, vaccine studies, longitudinal research, and behavioral interventions.
  • Commitment to Quality & Ethics – High standards for safety, data integrity, and ethical research practices.
  • Community Engagement – Strong partnerships with local communities enhance participant recruitment, retention, and study impact.

Past Successes

  • Leadership in international COVID-19 therapeutic trials that informed Long COVID treatment strategies.

Barbara S. Taylor, MD, MS

Director
Center for Chronic Infectious Diseases

Dr. Barbara Taylor is a Professor of Infectious Diseases, the founding Director of the Center for Chronic Infectious Diseases, and the Assistant Dean for the MD/MPH Program at UT Health San Antonio. Dr. Taylor conducts research to improve health outcomes for those living with or at risk for HIV, with a specific focus on Latinx populations, and is an infectious disease specialist with a focus on care of people living with HIV and other viral illnesses. Her current work uses implementation science to address barriers to care for emerging adults with HIV in South Texas and explores risk for STIs in key populations in the Dominican Republic. She is also a principal investigator on the NIH-funded RECOVER study, which works to determine the impact of long COVID in the United States. She is passionate about fostering research that improves the health of Texans and eliminates chronic infectious diseases and their sequelae. In her spare time, she loves walking along the river walk with spouse and her tiny yorkie, reading, and being a sports mom to her two amazing daughters.
