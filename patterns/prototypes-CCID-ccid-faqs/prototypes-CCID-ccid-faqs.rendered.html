About Participation

Q: What is clinical research?
A: Clinical research is the process of studying health and disease in people to find new ways to prevent, diagnose, and treat medical conditions. Research studies help us learn what works best and ensure new treatments are safe and effective.

Q: Why should I consider participating in a research study?
A: By joining a study, you contribute to discoveries that can improve health for your community and beyond. Many participants also gain access to new treatments, closer health monitoring, and the chance to work with a dedicated research team.

Q: Do I need to have a specific medical condition to join a study?
A: Not always. Some studies are for people with specific conditions, while others need healthy volunteers. Each study has its own eligibility criteria, which we’ll review with you.

Q: What types of research does your Center conduct?
A: We focus on chronic infectious diseases that affect South Texas, including HIV, tuberculosis, fungal infections, and Long COVID. Our studies include clinical trials of new treatments, vaccine research, behavioral interventions, and community-based health studies.

Safety & Confidentiality

Q: How is my safety protected during a study?
A: Your safety is our top priority. All studies are reviewed and approved by ethics committees, and you’ll be closely monitored by trained medical staff throughout the process.

Q: Who oversees the ethical conduct of your research?
A: Every study is reviewed by an Institutional Review Board (IRB), which ensures research is conducted ethically and safely.

Q: Will my personal information remain confidential?
A: Yes. We follow strict federal and institutional privacy rules to protect your information. Only approved members of the research team will have access to your study data.

Logistics

Q: How do I find out which studies are currently enrolling?
A: Visit our Research Studies page or contact our team directly. We’ll be happy to share information about ongoing studies and check if you’re eligible.

Q: What happens during a typical research visit?
A: It depends on the study, but visits may include a health screening, answering questionnaires, lab tests, or receiving a treatment. We’ll explain everything in advance and answer all your questions.

Q: Will I be compensated for my time or travel?
A: Many of our studies offer compensation for your time, travel, or other expenses. Details vary by study and will be provided during the consent process.

Q: Do I have to come to your Center, or can some studies be done at home?
A: Some studies require in-person visits, while others may include phone calls, online surveys, or home-based participation. We’ll let you know what options are available.

Rights & Commitment

Q: Can I stop participating in a study once I start?
A: Yes. Participation is completely voluntary, and you can withdraw at any time without penalty or loss of medical care.

Q: What are my rights as a research participant?
A: You have the right to be fully informed, to ask questions, to withdraw at any time, and to have your privacy protected. Your rights will be explained in detail before you agree to join any study.

Q: What should I do if I have questions or concerns during a study?
A: You can reach out to the research team at any time. We will provide you with direct contact information before your study begins.

Community Engagement

Q: How does your Center work with South Texas communities?
A: We partner closely with local organizations, clinics, and community leaders to ensure our research addresses real health needs and respects community values.

Q: How does participating in research help improve health in my community?
A: Every study helps us better understand infectious diseases and develop new solutions. This knowledge directly benefits South Texas and contributes to national and global health.

Q: Can community organizations partner with your Center?
A: Yes! We welcome partnerships with schools, clinics, nonprofits, and other organizations that share our mission. Contact us to explore opportunities.

Other Practical Questions

Q: Do you provide services in Spanish or other languages?
A: Yes. Many of our staff are bilingual, and interpreter services are available to ensure you can participate comfortably in your preferred language.

Q: Where are you located, and is parking available?
A: We are located at [insert building/UT Health San Antonio address]. Visitor parking is available nearby, and we will provide details when you schedule your first visit.

Q: Who can I contact if I’m interested in learning more?
A: You can complete the form on the Contact Us page. Our research team will be happy to answer your questions!
