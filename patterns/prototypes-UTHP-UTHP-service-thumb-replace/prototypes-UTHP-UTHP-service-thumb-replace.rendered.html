Thumb Replacement

Orthopaedics

What is thumb replacement (thumb arthroplasty)?

Thumb replacement surgery replaces a damaged thumb joint with artificial (prosthetic) implants. Thumb carpometacarpal (CMC) arthroplasty and thumb arthroplasty are the medical terms for thumb replacement surgery. Your healthcare provider may recommend surgery if hand pain doesn’t improve with nonsurgical treatments.

Orthopaedic hand surgeons at UT Health San Antonio were among the first in the country to offer thumb arthroplasty after the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approved the implants in 2025. This approval came after a decade of successful use in European patients.

What is thumb arthritis?

The CMC joint at the base of your thumb is a type of saddle, or interlocking, joint. It allows you to rotate your thumb in different directions so you can grasp, pinch and hold items. The CMC joint is especially prone to osteoarthritis. This condition occurs when cartilage tissue breaks down due to use and age.

Cartilage helps bones glide smoothly at a joint. When you have thumb arthritis, bone begins to rub against bone. You may find it painful or difficult to open doors or jars, button a shirt or hold a glass. Thumb arthroplasty helps restore pain-free hand movements.

  • Experts in advanced hand surgeries: You receive care from fellowship-trained orthopaedic surgeons who specialize in hand surgeries. Our hand surgeons completed specific training with experts in Europe who have been performing thumb arthroplasty for a decade.
  • Focus on improving patient outcomes: We were selected as one of four centers in the country to participate in an FDA study to track the outcomes of patients who receive thumb joint replacements. Our experts are helping to shape the future standard of care for people with thumb arthritis nationwide.
  • Trusted, comprehensive joint care: Patients from across the country and globe come to us for total joint care. We offer the latest nonsurgical and surgical treatments to help you stay mobile and enjoy an active life.
  • Ongoing care: We provide lifelong follow-up care. You may see your surgeon annually to ensure the implanted joint continues to function as it should./li>

What to expect with thumb replacement

To determine whether joint replacement is right for you, your orthopaedic surgeon will:

  • Ask about your symptoms
  • Perform a physical exam to assess your range of motion
  • Order X-rays to look for joint damage

Thumb joint replacement is an outpatient procedure, so you go home the same day. Most patients can resume all usual activities six weeks after surgery.

Preparing for thumb replacement

Experts at our perioperative optimization clinic work with you to ensure a safe surgical experience and recovery. Before surgery, you may:

  • Get blood tests, heart tests and additional imaging
  • See a cardiologist, rheumatologist, endocrinologist or other specialist for help managing conditions that can affect surgery and recovery
  • Seek help to quit tobacco products, which can slow healing
  • Stop taking certain medications and supplements that increase the risk of bleeding

What to expect during thumb replacement

You’ll first receive anesthesia to make you sleep through the surgery. During the procedure, your orthopaedic hand surgeon:

  • Makes a 2-inch incision near the wrist at the base of your thumb
  • Removes a small section of the metacarpal bone that connects your wrist and thumb
  • Inserts an implant with a stem into the remaining metacarpal bone
  • Makes a small hole in the wrist bone at the base of the thumb (trapezium) and places a cup-shaped implant (socket) into the opening
  • Uses a ball-and-socket implant to connect the other two implants
  • Closes the incision

What to expect after thumb replacement

Thumb implants are porous, which means new bone gradually grows into them to stabilize and strengthen the joint. But this takes time. You’ll have limited use of your thumb and hand for about six weeks.

After surgery, you’ll go home with a splint and bandages covering your thumb. Your care team will give you follow-up instructions, which may require you to:

  • Take pain relievers, as prescribed
  • Keep your thumb immobile for the first week
  • Remove the bandage after one week and follow your provider’s instructions to care for the incision site
  • Participate in hand physical therapy starting one week after surgery to help you regain joint strength and mobility
  • See your provider in two weeks for X-rays, at which point you may be able to use the thumb for light activities like dressing and feeding yourself
  • Gradually return to normal activities, such as lifting, pulling, pushing and grasping, after six weeks

Benefits of thumb replacement

With thumb joint replacement, most people see significant pain relief and improved movement in just a few weeks. The procedure can:

  • Ease chronic joint pain, stiffness and inflammation
  • Get you back to using your hands and participating in physical activities faster
  • Help you complete daily tasks without pain
  • Improve range of motion in your thumb and your overall hand function

Risks of thumb joint replacement

Complications can occur with any surgical procedure. Risk of thumb replacement include complications such as:

  • Blood clots or bleeding
  • Bone fractures
  • Implant dislocation or allergic reaction
  • Infection
  • Nerve damage
  • Reaction to anesthesia

Thumb joint replacement vs. trapeziectomy

Thumb joint replacement is a welcome alternative to the standard surgical treatment for severe thumb arthritis, called trapeziectomy. A trapeziectomy removes the entire trapezium bone and does not replace it with an implant.

While this procedure stops bones from rubbing together, taking out the trapezium often makes the thumb shorter and weaker. This change affects grip strength. Recovery is also longer — returning to your usual activities can take up to six months.

Most people are familiar with hip replacement. This is that same concept applied to the base of the thumb, on a much smaller scale, with the goal of preserving strength and allowing earlier return to daily activities.
